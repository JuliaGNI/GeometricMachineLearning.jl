Multi-Modal Symplectic Autoencoders

A MultiModalSymplecticAutoencoder maps several descriptions of one phase-space state into one latent space $\xi = (\tilde{q}, \tilde{p}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2n}$. Each description, or modality, has its own frontend layer, and all frontends share one symplectic core. This page builds such a network for a 1D1V system with three modalities, and trains it in three stages. The script scripts/reproduction/multimodal_symplectic_autoencoder.jl runs every step of this page in Float32 and in Float64, and asserts the claim of each stage.

The dataset is synthetic

The snapshots on this page are built from two spatial modes with fixed velocity profiles, plus noise. They test that the pipeline runs and that each stage does what it claims. They say nothing about the physics: whether a shared latent space reproduces a Vlasov or a particle simulation better than a linear reduced basis is the question of a separate benchmark on Landau damping and the two-stream instability, not of this page.

The architecture

The network is a NamedTuple of frontends and a core:

using GeometricMachineLearning
using Random: Xoshiro

frontends = (grid = BilinearStiefelLayer(16, 16, 2, 2),
    dlra = LowRankStiefelLayer(16, 16, 2, 2; r_out = 4),
    particles = CanonicalSlotLayer(8, 1, 4))
core = WeightTiedSymplecticAutoencoder(8, 4; n_blocks = 1, n_layers = 2,
    n_ambient_layers = 0, sympnet_upscale = 4)
nn = NeuralNetwork(MultiModalSymplecticAutoencoder(frontends, core), CPU(), Float64;
    rng = Xoshiro(42))

Each frontend maps its modality to $2N = 8$ frontend coordinates, the input dimension of the core, and the core maps them to $2n = 4$ latent coordinates.

  • The grid frontend, BilinearStiefelLayer(Nx, Nv, mx, mv), takes a field $F \in \mathbb{R}^{N_x \times N_v}$, splits it into its even and odd parts in $v$, and projects both onto Stiefel bases $\Phi_x$ and $\Phi_v$. A spline modality is the same layer with metric_factors = (Rx, Rv), the factors of the mass matrices of the spline bases.
  • The DLRA frontend, LowRankStiefelLayer, takes a factored state $(X, S, V)$ with $F = X S V^T$ and, with the same bases $\Phi_x$ and $\Phi_v$, gives the coordinates that the grid frontend gives for $F$, without assembling $F$.
  • The particle frontend, CanonicalSlotLayer(M, d, NSlots), takes $M$ particles in $d$ dimensions, sorts them, and keeps their centre of mass and the projection of their sorted relative modes onto $N_{\mathrm{slots}} - 1$ slots with a Stiefel basis $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$.

The frontend coordinates are canonical for the structure $\mathbb{J}_{2N}$ that each frontend declares on them, and the symplectic core relates them to latent coordinates canonical for $\mathbb{J}_{2n}$. The two parity frontends are linear in $F$, so they are not Poisson maps for the Vlasov bracket; the docstring of BilinearStiefelLayer says more.

encode(nn, modality, x) applies a frontend and the core's encoder, decode(nn, modality, ξ) applies the core's decoder and the inverse of a frontend, and translate(nn, from, to, x) is decode(nn, to, encode(nn, from, x)).

The core is a WeightTiedSymplecticAutoencoder: its encoder is the exact inverse of its decoder layers, applied to the same parameters. Its decode–encode is therefore the projection of its last PSDLayer, whatever the gradient layers between, and the reconstruction error of the network is set by its linear parts: the bases of the frontend and the symplectic lift of the core.

The data

The script draws $B = 8$ snapshots in smoke mode and $B = 32$ in full mode, all with Random.Xoshiro(42). A snapshot is $F_b = f_0 + \delta F_b$: a background $f_0$, even in $v$, and a fluctuation $\delta F_b$ of two spatial modes, each with an even and an odd velocity profile, plus noise of size $10^{-3}$. The DLRA snapshots are the rank-4 truncated SVDs of the fluctuations. The frontends are linear, so the network sees the fluctuations, and the background returns in Stage III as the reference state.

Stage I: subspaces from the SVD

solve! sets the linear parts of the network from singular value decompositions, with no training: the Stiefel bases of the grid frontend, and the cotangent lift in the core's last PSDLayer, from the frontend coordinates of the snapshots:

using GeometricMachineLearning: params
using GeometricOptimizers: solve!

ps = params(nn)
solve!(nn.architecture.frontends.grid, ps.frontends.grid, δF)   # Φx and Φv of the grid

psd = NeuralNetwork(PSDArch(8, 4), CPU(), Float64; rng = Xoshiro(42))
solve!(psd, Z)                                                    # the cotangent lift of Z
copyto!(parent(ps.core[end].weight), parent(params(psd)[1].weight))

Here δF holds the fluctuations, of size (16, 16, B), and Z holds their frontend coordinates, one column per snapshot. Stage I does not fit the DLRA frontend. Its bases $\Phi_x$ and $\Phi_v$ are those of the grid frontend plus $0.1$ times a random matrix, orthonormalized, so that Stage II has the two modalities to align. The script asserts that the projection error

\[\varepsilon_I = \max_b \frac{\|F_b - (f_0 + \Pi(F_b - f_0))\|}{\|F_b\|},\]

with $\Pi$ the decode–encode of the grid modality through the whole network, is below $10^{-2}$. Without Stage I it is about $0.15$.

Stage II: joint training

Stage II trains all modalities together. Its loss is the sum of three terms over the batch: the CharbonnierRelativeLoss of the grid modality, the CrossModalLoss from the DLRA modality to the grid modality, and the relative squared error of the particle states that translate(nn, :grid, :particles, δF) gives. The particle term translates rather than encodes: the particle frontend sorts its input, and a decoded particle state need not be in the order that the sort keeps.

The CrossModalLoss aligns the DLRA frontend, which Stage I does not fit, with the grid frontend. The particle states of the synthetic dataset are built with a relative basis that is not the network's, so Stage II also has to learn $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$. The optimizer steps the Stiefel leaves with FactoredStiefelAdam, a direct tangent-space Adam of GeometricOptimizers that keeps each leaf on the manifold, and all other leaves with Adam. With loss(model, ps) the sum of the three terms, the training loop of the smoke mode is:

using GeometricOptimizers: TrainingOptimizer, CompositeMethod, FactoredStiefelAdam, Adam,
                           optimization_step!
using Zygote: Zygote

optimizer = TrainingOptimizer(params(nn);
    algorithm = CompositeMethod(; manifold = FactoredStiefelAdam(), array = Adam()),
    linesearch = 1e-3)
for step in 1:400
    dp = Zygote.gradient(p -> loss(nn.model, p), params(nn))[1]
    optimization_step!(params(nn), optimizer, dp)
end

See TrainingOptimizer and CompositeMethod. The script prints, after every step, the sum of the reconstruction and the CrossModalLoss terms, without the particle term. It asserts two things: that this sum after the last step is at most half its value after Stage I, and that every StiefelManifold leaf $Y$ satisfies $\|Y^T Y - I\|_F \le 50\,\mathrm{eps}(T)$. Adam is not a descent method, so the loss need not fall at every step, and the script does not assert that it does. Euclidean Adam on the Stiefel leaves breaks the second assertion.

Stage III: the reduced system

Stage III integrates the reduced system of a Hamiltonian on the frontend coordinates with HRedSys. The core's encoder and decoder reduce the $2N = 8$ frontend coordinates to $2n = 4$, and the reference state q_ref, p_ref is the frontend coordinates of the background $f_0$:

using GeometricIntegrators: ImplicitMidpoint, StateVariable

z_ref = frontend_coordinates(f0)
z0 = frontend_coordinates(F0)
ics = (q = StateVariable(z0[1:4]), p = StateVariable(z0[5:8]))
rs = HRedSys(8, 4, encoder(nn), decoder(nn), v!, f!, h, (0.0, 2.0), 2.0 / 20, ics;
    integrator = ImplicitMidpoint(), q_ref = z_ref[1:4], p_ref = z_ref[5:8],
    pullback_mode = :vjp)
sol = integrate_reduced_system(rs)

Here frontend_coordinates(F) is the output (q, p) of the grid frontend as one vector, and v!, f! and h are the vector fields and the energy of a pendulum on each coordinate, $H(q, p) = \frac{1}{2}|p|^2 + \sum_i (1 - \cos q_i)$. The script asserts two things:

  • The Hamiltonian is not quadratic, so the implicit midpoint rule conserves it only to second order in the time step. Over the interval $(0, 2)$, the largest error of the reduced energy in 20 steps is between 3 and 5 times the error in 40 steps.
  • The decoded initial field $f_0 + \mathrm{field}(\mathrm{decode}(\mathrm{encode}(z_0)) - z_{\mathrm{ref}})$ equals $F_0$ to $\varepsilon_I \|F_0\|$. Without the shift, with no q_ref, p_ref and no $f_0$ added on decoding, the background is not in the range of the rank-$2n$ decoder, and the relative error is about 1.

The script integrates a fixed latent Hamiltonian and trains no latent dynamics. To fine-tune a latent integrator on decoded trajectories, use IsometricRolloutLoss.

Run time

The script prints the time from the end of using to the end of Stage III, compilation included. It asserts no limit on it. Almost all of the time is compilation: the first gradient of the Stage II loss and the first integration of each precision.
